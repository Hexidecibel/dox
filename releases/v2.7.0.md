## The portal stops taking a document's word for it

Everything since v2.6.0 — 152 commits spanning the document registry, the COA
record split, the teach loop, extraction measurement, and spec limits.

The through-line is trust. dox could already read a document and file it; it
had no opinion about whether what it read was right, and no way to say so.

- **Spec limits** judge a COA's test results against an acceptance range —
  the limit printed on the document, and the tighter one you hold. Three-state
  on purpose: in spec, out of spec, and *could not check*. A censored "<50"
  against a 10 CFU/g limit could be 2 or 49, and calling that either way would
  be a lie. Verdicts persist with a frozen copy of the limit they were judged
  against, so moving a threshold cannot rewrite history.
- **Extraction invariants** check each value against the document's own text —
  no model, no answer key — and warn the reviewer inline at the field, before
  they approve rather than after.
- **Measured accuracy** replaced guesswork: 90.6% corpus-wide (85.1–93.5%),
  graded from reviewer decisions instead of a hand-marked sample.
- **Geometry-aware PDF serialization** repaired a text layer that was being
  destroyed before the model ever saw it.

Alongside that, the **document registry** gained its taxonomy — what a document
IS, what it SATISFIES, what it TRIGGERS — plus a renewal engine and
natural-language retrieval. COAs now split properly into per-lot and per-sublot
records, with per-supplier lot schemes and a product bridge taught at review
time. The **teach loop** became conversational and grounded in what the system
already knows, over a two-layer extraction context each tenant can edit.

Nothing files itself. Every one of these warns and asks for a human.
