## What the page already said

Two facts are printed on nearly every specification sheet, and extraction had
nowhere to put either of them. How long the product keeps, and the document's
own number. They were on the page the whole time; they simply fell on the floor
between the fields we asked for. Reading them changed the score more than the
last three accuracy passes put together, and it removed an invented date in the
process.

### Added

- [existing] Extraction now reads a product's shelf life and a document's own number. Shelf life is kept exactly as the page prints it, including its condition — "1 year frozen, 21 days refrigerated" stays whole rather than being reduced to a number we would have had to choose.
- [existing] A document number is told apart from a lot, a purchase order, an item code and a revision, and on a certificate the certificate's own number is used instead.
- [existing] On the client's own documents this took extraction from 89% to 96% of values read correctly, and removed a case where an expiry date was being invented.

### Changed

- _nothing_

### Internal

- No migration. The two fields land in the existing `primary_metadata` JSON, so nothing about the schema moves.
- The extraction schema, the field aliases and all three mirrored prompt copies change together: `BASE_PROMPT` in `functions/lib/llm.ts` plus the text and VLM prompts in `bin/process-worker`. The mirror test pins them to each other.
- Shelf life is deliberately NOT a renewal input and NOT a date key. It is a period, and the period tiers in `shared/renewalPeriod.ts` would happily read "21 days" as a proposal to re-collect a specification sheet three weeks after it was issued — when a spec sheet renews at three years because of what it is. A shelf life is a fact about the product; how long the paperwork is good for is a fact about the paperwork. Pinned by `tests/unit/shelfLifeNotRenewal.test.ts`, with the same guard written into `shared/extractionInvariants.ts` so it is never parsed as a calendar date.
- Measured on the real corpus: value accuracy 89.4% to 96.5%, and 94.4% to 96.3% like-for-like on the fields that were already being graded, with one fabricated expiry gone. Doctype corpus 38/40, value accuracy 95.7%.
- The prompt change reaches only documents extracted after the processing worker is next restarted; it is not part of the Pages deploy.
